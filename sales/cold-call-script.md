# Cold Call Script: Returnline

**This call isn't a sales call.** You only want one thing from it: a 10-minute visit or a quick call to count their missed calls. That count is the first step of the free trial. You aren't selling anything on this call.

> **YOUR DECISION REQUIRED (Jordan):** this is customer-facing. Read it out loud once, change anything that doesn't sound like you, and approve it before the first dial.
>
> Note: as of 2026-09-28, calling is a main channel alongside email (audit kit) and drop-offs. Use it with the after-hours test result as the hook whenever you have one.

## 1. Before you dial

**The 3-line checklist** (write it on a sticky note for each shop):
1. **Who:** `[OWNER NAME]` (or "ask for the owner" if you don't know it)
2. **Hook:** `[DAY]` `[TIME]`, `[TEST RESULT]` (or "no hook")
3. **The ask:** a 10-minute visit. Have two times ready.

**Times you can offer for the visit** (from your schedule):
- Mon 1:00-4:00
- Wed 12:15-2:30
- Fri 12:15-3:30

**When to call.** You can only call midday:
- Mon / Wed / Fri: 12:15-4:00
- Tue / Thu: 1:00-4:00

**Be honest with yourself about midday.** HVAC owners are often out on jobs or driving between them in the afternoon, so midday calls usually get the office manager or front desk. That's fine. Plan for it:
- **Goal 1:** reach the owner. **Goal 2:** get the owner's best time and a direct number or cell. **Goal 3:** get the office manager interested. They're the ones hearing the phone ring while they're on another line.
- If they say the owner is "usually in first thing" or "in after 4," you can't make those times. So ask for the direct line instead, or ask to book the 10-minute visit through the office manager.
- A good afternoon to try is right after lunch (about 1:00-1:30), when some owners are back in the office. Treat that as a guess to test, not a rule. Write down what actually works per shop.

**Dial by hand, one call at a time.** If anyone says "don't call us again," say "Understood, I won't," and mark the shop **do not contact** in `pipeline.md` the same day.

## 2. Gatekeeper script (office manager or front desk)

**Opening:**
> "Hi, this is Jordan with Returnline. I'm local in Conway. Is `[OWNER NAME]` around?"

If you don't know the owner's name:
> "Hi, this is Jordan with Returnline. I'm local in Conway. Who's the owner there? ... Great, is `[OWNER NAME]` around?"

**If they ask what it's about:**
> "I help HVAC shops catch the calls they miss. I just want to ask `[OWNER NAME]` for 10 minutes to count their missed calls. It's free."

**If the owner is there:** go to the owner script (section 3).

**If the owner isn't available:**
> "No problem. When's the best time to catch them?"
>
> "Is there a direct number or cell that's better for them?"

If the best time is outside your hours:
> "I can't call in the mornings. Could I book 10 minutes with them on `[DAY]` at `[TIME]` instead?"

**Short pitch to the office manager** (use it if they sound friendly or say "I handle that"):
> "Can I ask you, since you're the one on the phones? When you're on one line and another call comes in, or it rings after you close, what happens to that call?"

Let them answer. Then:
> "That's what I help with. When a call gets missed, the customer gets a text back in under a minute, and the job request comes to you to confirm. It starts with a free two-week trial, and step one is just counting the missed calls from your call log. Would you or `[OWNER NAME]` have 10 minutes on `[DAY]` or `[DAY]`?"

**Before you hang up, always get:** the owner's name, the best time, a direct line if they'll give it, and the office manager's name.

## 3. Owner script (about 30 seconds)

**Permission opener:**
> "Hi `[OWNER NAME]`, this is Jordan with Returnline. I'm local in Conway. I know I'm calling out of the blue. Can I have 30 seconds, and then you tell me if it's worth talking?"

Wait for a yes. If they say no, go to "I'm busy" in section 5.

**Hook, version A (use it ONLY if your test call really happened and it's in your log):**
> "I called `[SHOP NAME]` on `[DAY]` at `[TIME]`, and `[TEST RESULT]`."
>
> Add this only if they advertise it: "I saw you offer `[24/7 CLAIM]`, so I figured you'd want to know."
>
> "When that happens, a lot of callers don't leave a message. They just call the next shop."

**Hook, version B (no test call, or a person answered it):**
> "I help HVAC shops around here catch the calls they miss when techs are on a job or the office is closed. When a call goes to voicemail, a lot of callers don't leave a message. They just call the next shop."

**Discovery question (pick one, then stop talking):**
> "How many calls do you think your company misses in a month?"

Let them answer. Don't argue with the number.

**The ask:**
> "I'm not selling anything today. I'd like 10 minutes to count your missed calls from your call log. It's free. Could I stop by `[DAY]` at `[TIME]`, or `[DAY]` at `[TIME]`?"

If they'd rather not have a visit:
> "No problem. Could we do a quick 10-minute call instead? `[DAY]` or `[DAY]`?"

**When they say yes:** repeat the day and time back, get their cell number or email, and say "Thanks. See you `[DAY]`." Then hang up. Don't keep pitching after the yes.

## 4. Discovery questions (only if they want to talk)

Ask one at a time. Listen more than you talk. Write down what they say.

1. "When a call gets missed right now, what happens to it?"
2. "How many calls do you think you miss in a month?"
3. "How do jobs get booked? Does the office schedule them, or the techs?"
4. "What's a typical job worth to you? A repair call, and a new system?"
5. "Who follows up with people who left a voicemail or called and hung up?"

Then go back to the ask: "Want to count the real number? Could I come by `[DAY]` or `[DAY]`?"

## 5. Objection answers

Keep it short. Answer, then ask once more. If it's still no, thank them and let it go.

**"Not interested."**
> "Understood, thanks for telling me straight. Have a good one."
(Don't push. Log it. Don't call back unless they reach out.)

**"We answer all our calls."**
> "That's great. The count takes 10 minutes and it's free. If the number's low, you know you're covered. Want to check?"

**"How much?"** (check `pipeline.md` first to see if founding spots are open)
> "Regular price is $497 a month, no setup fee. My first three shops get $397 a month, locked for 12 months, for a case study. It's month to month after a free two-week trial. And if we don't recover one real job in your first 30 paid days, that month's free."

(A "real job" is a booked appointment from a new-customer lead that came through our system. If they ask, say exactly that.)

**"Send me an email."**
> "Happy to. What's the best email? And so it's not just another email, could I put down `[DAY]` for 10 minutes, and you can cancel if it doesn't look worth it?"

(Send it the same day. Use the "Send more info" reply in `audit-outreach-kit.md`.)

**"I'm busy."**
> "Totally get it, I'll be quick. When's a better time to call back, `[DAY]` or `[DAY]`?"

**"We already have an answering service."**
> "Good, that means you care about the calls. This doesn't replace them. It texts back the calls that still slip through, and the free count would show if any do. Worth 10 minutes to check?"

**"Is this AI?"**
> "Yes. The texts are written by AI, with strict rules. It asks what's wrong, how urgent it is, and what time works. It never quotes prices or diagnoses anything, and your office confirms every appointment. Anything like a gas smell or CO alarm gets told to call 911 or the gas company, and a real person gets alerted."

**"How old are you?" / "How long have you been doing this?"**
> "I'm 20, a student here in Conway, and Returnline is new. You'd be one of my first shops. That's why the trial is free, and why there's a guarantee: no real job in the first paid month, that month's free. I'd rather earn it."

## 6. Voicemail (under 20 seconds)

Only leave one if it's the owner's direct line or cell, or the office asked you to. Otherwise hang up and call back another day.

> "Hi `[OWNER NAME]`, this is Jordan with Returnline, local in Conway. I help HVAC shops catch missed calls. I'd like 10 minutes to count yours for free. My number is `[PHONE]`. Again, `[PHONE]`. Thanks."

Don't mention the test-call result on a voicemail. Save it for when you have them live.

## 8. Confidence notes

- **Stand up when you dial.** Your voice sounds steadier on your feet. Smile before they pick up. They can hear it.
- **It's fine to read the script.** Nobody can see your paper. Reading it well beats making it up.
- **A "no" is data, not a verdict on you.** Log it and move on. Every no tells us something about the shop, the time or the niche.
- **Aim for 10 dials, not 10 yeses.** You control the dials. Getting through 10 is a win, even if nobody books.
- **You're not asking for money.** You're offering a free 10-minute count. If it's not for them, that's okay, and you can say so.
