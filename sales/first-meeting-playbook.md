# First meeting playbook (10 minutes)

The goal is a "yes" to the free 2-week trial, and you get there by **listening more than you pitch**.

## Bring
- Your phone with the **preview page** open: the "Returnline Missed-Call Preview" artifact, a walkthrough of a missed call becoming a job request. Say plainly that it's an example.
- 2 printed one-pagers.
- A notepad with the intake questions below.

## Be honest about where we are
There's no live system until a shop says yes. Say it straight:
> "You'd be one of my first shops. That's why the trial is free and there's a guarantee. I set it up for you, you watch it work for two weeks, and then you decide."

Once the GoHighLevel demo is built, you'll also have a live version: they call a number and get a real text back.

## The 10 minutes
| Minute | What you do | Words |
|---|---|---|
| 0-2 | **Ask, don't pitch** | "How do calls get handled when you're out on jobs, or after 5?" · "What happens to the people who don't leave a message?" |
| 2-4 | **Count together** | "Could we pull up your missed calls from last week on your phone or phone system and count them?" Write the number down. **This number sells it, not you.** |
| 4-6 | **Show the preview** | Hand them your phone and press Play. "This is what your caller would see, and this is what lands on your phone." |
| 6-8 | **Explain the trial** | "One setting on your phone. Your number stays the same. Your office confirms every job. Two weeks free. After that it's $397 a month for my first three shops, and if we don't bring you at least one real job in your first paid month, that month is free." |
| 8-10 | **Ask** | "Want to try it free for two weeks?" |

## If they say yes
1. "Great. Can I grab a few details so I can set it up?" Get: business hours, service area (towns or ZIP codes), emergency or on-call contact, how jobs get booked now, whether prices can be quoted (default: no), and **who gets the job alerts, and how** (owner or office, text, email, app or a morning summary), plus the on-call phone for emergencies.
2. Book the setup call: "I'll text you when it's ready to switch on. It takes about 5 minutes on your phone."
3. Text Claude right away. The team starts the setup (texting registration, the AI settings, safety testing).

## If they say "I don't want these texts on my phone"
"No problem, you pick where they go:
- your office manager's or dispatcher's phone instead of yours
- email, or an app where your office can see and answer the conversations
- or nothing at night, just one summary at 7am of everything that came in overnight.

The one thing that can't be switched off: if a customer says they smell gas or their CO alarm is going off, the on-call person gets an alert right away. That can be a tech instead of you."

Write down their choice as an intake detail.

## If they say "not now"
"Totally fair. Can I check back in two weeks?" Log it in the pipeline with the date.

## Don't
- Don't promise numbers ("you'll get 10 more jobs"). We don't have results yet.
- Don't say "AI" first. Say "instant text-back." If they ask whether it's AI: "Yes, an automated assistant handles the texting. It never quotes prices or diagnoses anything, and emergencies go straight to you."
- Don't sign anything yet. For the free trial, a text or email summary of what you agreed is enough. The written agreement comes after a lawyer reviews it, before any paid month.
