# Audit-First Outreach Kit: Returnline

Outreach that doesn't need a walk-in pitch or a cold call.

**How it works:** you call a shop after hours (see `after-hours-test-calls.md`) and log what actually happened. That real result is the reason you're reaching out. Then you email or mail them. You only show up in person after they've already heard from you.

> **YOUR DECISION REQUIRED (Jordan):** these are customer-facing messages. Read every piece out loud, change anything that doesn't sound like you, and approve it before the first send.

**Use the hook only when:**
- The result was voicemail, rang out, or disconnected. If an answering service picked up, say exactly that ("an answering service took a message") and nothing more. If a person answered, **don't use this kit on that shop.** They pick up, so there's nothing honest to say here.
- You're sending within about a week of the call. Older than that, call again and use the new result.
- You only add `[CALLBACK]` if you actually waited and checked. Otherwise, leave it out.

## 1. Cold email

**Subject line options** (use one, lowercase is fine):
1. `called [SHOP NAME] [DAY] night`
2. `your after-hours calls`
3. `quick question about missed calls`

**Body** (plain text: no images, no links, no attachments. Body is about 110 words, not counting the footer):

```
Hi [FIRST NAME],

I called [SHOP NAME] on [DAY] at [TIME], and [RESULT]. I'm Jordan, a student here in Conway, and I've been checking which local HVAC shops pick up after hours.

I help shops text those callers back in under a minute, find out what they need, and send the job request to your office to confirm. It starts with a free 2-week trial. First we count your missed calls from your call log, so you see the real number yourself.

If it's worth a look, just reply here. I can also do a 10-minute call or stop by.

How many calls like that do you think slip through in a month?

Jordan
Returnline
[PHONE]

--
Returnline · [MAILING ADDRESS]
Don't want emails from me? Reply "stop" and I won't email you again.
```

## 3. Facebook message

Use this only for shops with an active Facebook page (recent posts, or a "typically replies" badge) and no public email address. About 65 words.

```
Hi, this is Jordan with Returnline. I'm a student here in Conway. I called [SHOP NAME] on [DAY] at [TIME] after hours, and [RESULT]. I help HVAC shops text back the callers they miss, so the job request still gets to the office. There's a free 2-week trial, and it starts by counting your missed calls. How many calls like that do you think slip through in a month?
```

**Google Business Profile: don't use it for this.** Google removed Business Profile chat on July 31, 2024. Today the "message" option on a profile may open a text message or WhatsApp to the shop's phone. That would be cold texting, which the Rules box says not to do. If their profile links to a website with a contact form, you can paste the cold email body (section 1) into that form.

## 5. Warm drop-off (only AFTER an email or letter)

About 20 seconds. No pitch. You're dropping off a page and asking for a time.

**Don't drop off** at shops that replied "not interested" or "stop." Drop off once per shop, and no more than that.

```
"Hi, I'm Jordan with Returnline. I emailed [FIRST NAME] about the call I made here [DAY] night that [RESULT].
I just wanted to drop off the one-pager in person.
When's a good time for 10 minutes this week?"
```

- **If they ask what it is:** *"When you miss a call, it texts the customer back and sends the job request to your office. It starts with a free trial."* Then stop talking.
- **If the owner isn't in:** leave the one-pager, then ask *"Who's the best person for this, and when are they usually in?"*
- **Right after:** log the contact's name, the best time to reach them, and the date you dropped off.

## 6. Reply handling

Each reply ends with one question, except "not interested" (see why below). Answer the same day if you can.

**"How much?"**
Check `pipeline.md` first. Use the founding rate only while fewer than 3 founding clients have signed.

```
Fair question. The regular price is $497 a month with no setup fee. I'm taking my first three shops at $397 a month, locked for 12 months, no setup fee, in exchange for a case study. It's month to month, and you get the 2-week trial free first. If we don't recover at least one real job in your first 30 paid days, that month is free.

Want to start with the free missed-call count this week?
```

(A "real job" means a booked appointment from a new-customer lead that came through our system. If they ask, say exactly that.)

**"Not interested"**

```
Understood, thanks for letting me know. I won't email you again. Good luck this season.
```

Leave the question off here on purpose. Asking one after a no reads as pushy. Mark the shop **do not contact** in `pipeline.md`, and handle "stop" or "unsubscribe" the same way.

**"Send more info"**
Attach the one-pager PDF. This is the one reply where an attachment is fine, because they asked for it.

```
Here's the one-pager. Short version: when you miss a call, we text the customer back in under a minute, find out what they need, and send the request to your office to confirm. The free trial starts by counting your missed calls, so you see the number before anything else.

Would [DAY] or [DAY] work for a 10-minute call to walk through it?
```

**"Call me"**

Email reply:
```
Sure, happy to. Is [NUMBER THEY GAVE / number on your website] the best one, and is [DAY] at [TIME] okay?
```

The call itself should take under 60 seconds before you start listening. This is a call they asked for, so it isn't cold.

```
"Hey [FIRST NAME], it's Jordan from Returnline, thanks for having me call.
Quick question first: how many calls do you think your company misses every month?"
  (Let them answer. Listen.)
"That's what the free trial checks. First we count your missed calls from your call log, so it's a real number.
Then we turn on the text-back for two weeks. When a call's missed, the customer gets a text in under a minute,
we find out what they need, and the request goes to your office to confirm. No cost during the trial.
Could we start with the call-log count this week?"
```

For objections, use the answers in `walk-in-script.md`. They work the same on the phone.

## 7. Rules box

> **Read before the first send. This is general guidance, not legal advice. For anything you're unsure about, ask a lawyer.**
>
> 1. **No cold texting to shop phone numbers.** Many small-shop numbers are cell phones. Sending marketing texts to a phone without consent can create risk under the TCPA and carrier rules. Don't text a shop unless they texted you first or asked you to. That includes the "message" button on Google profiles, which can open a text. Before any texting outreach, ask a lawyer.
> 2. **Email to business addresses is fine with basics in place.** Use publicly listed business emails only (on their website or Facebook page). Every email needs your real name, "Returnline," a real physical mailing address, an honest subject line and a one-line opt-out. Honor opt-outs the same day and log them in `pipeline.md`.
> 3. **Mailing address. YOUR DECISION REQUIRED.** Your home address, a PO box or a virtual mailbox. Cost is `[VERIFY]`. Whether a PO box meets the email-address requirement is `[VERIFY]`: ask a lawyer or check the FTC's CAN-SPAM guide before choosing.
> 4. **Deliverability.** Send at most **20 emails a day** from the new domain. Send them one at a time from jordan@ in Google Workspace, not through a bulk tool. Set up SPF, DKIM and DMARC before the first send. First emails are plain text, with no links, images or attachments.
> 5. **Only claim what you observed.** Use the exact day, time and result from your log. Never guess, round up or add a callback you didn't check. No invented numbers, clients or results. We have none yet.
> 6. **Two follow-ups, then stop.** One drop-off at most. "Not interested" and "stop" mean do not contact.
> 7. **Pricing check.** Before quoting $397, confirm in `pipeline.md` that founding spots are open.
