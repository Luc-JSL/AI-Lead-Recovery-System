# Cold calls: talk tracks, not scripts

**Don't memorize words. Know the 4 beats, and say them your own way every time.**

Why: sales teams consistently find that reps who *sound* scripted do much worse than reps who sound conversational, even when they use nearly the same words. The fix is a **framework you know by heart, in your own language**, not a paragraph you read. (Sources at the bottom.)

---

## The 4 beats (fits on a sticky note)

```
1. WHO + WHY + PERMISSION   "It's Jordan, local in Conway. Honest heads up, this is a cold call. Want to hear why, and you tell me if it's worth it?"
2. WHAT I SAW (real)        "I called your line late Sunday night..."
3. ONE NEUTRAL QUESTION     "...so I'm curious, what happens to those callers overnight?"  → then SHUT UP
4. SMALL ASK                "Worth 10 minutes? I could swing by Wednesday or Friday."
```

That's the whole call. Everything else is **listening and asking one follow-up.**

### Beat 1: who, why, permission
Say who you are, admit it's a cold call, and ask permission. Owners hate being tricked, so naming it lowers their guard. Pick whichever sounds like you:
- "Hey Todd, it's Jordan. I'm local here in Conway. Honest heads up, this is a cold call. Want to hear why I'm calling, and you can tell me if it's worth your time?"
- "Hey Todd, Jordan here, I'm a local guy in Conway. I'll be quick. Can I tell you why I called, and you tell me if it's worth talking?"

Gong's research on cold calls found that giving a clear **reason for the call** made them work noticeably better, which is why beat 1 ends with "why I'm calling."

**Skip "How have you been?"** Gong's data found it works well in their calls (mostly software sales). But you've never met these owners, and in a small town it can come off as fake. Say **"How's your Monday going?"** only if it's natural for you.

### Beat 2: what you saw (it must be true)
Use **your real test call.** It's the one thing no other caller has.
- "I called your line late Sunday night, and the voicemail said you'd call back in the morning."
- "I called a handful of Conway shops late Sunday night, and almost all of them go to a voicemail that calls back the next morning. Yours too."

Both are true. **4 of the 5 Conway shops you called did exactly that.**

### Beat 3: one neutral question (this is where the call happens)
You're not pitching. You're asking a question that lets **them** notice the problem. This is Josh Braun's "poke the bear" idea: a neutral question about how they handle something today, with no agenda and no leading. Pick one:
- "What happens to those overnight callers? Do most wait for your callback?"
- "How often do you call someone back in the morning and they've already booked someone else?"
- "How are you handling that right now?"

**Then stop talking.** Count to 3 in your head. The silence feels awkward to you, but it's where they start telling you their problem.

**Dig once** with whatever they say:
- "How often does that happen?"
- "When it does, what's that job usually worth?"
- "Has that always been the case, or is it worse in summer?"

### Beat 4: small ask
Only ask for 10 minutes, never for a sale:
- "Honestly, it might be worth 10 minutes to show you what I do about that. I could swing by Wednesday or Friday. Would either work?"
- "Want me to show you? Takes 10 minutes. I'm around Wednesday and Friday afternoons."

### If they say no
Stay relaxed and curious. You're not trying to win the call:
- "No worries at all. Mind if I ask, is it just not a problem for you, or is it timing?"
- If it's timing: "Fair. Could I check back in a couple of weeks?"
- If it's a real no: "Appreciate you being straight with me. Have a good one."

## How to not sound like a robot
1. **Talk like you'd talk to a friend's dad.** Short sentences, your own words.
2. **Slow down.** Nerves make everyone talk fast. Aim for about half the speed that feels natural.
3. **Stand up and smile.** It honestly changes your voice.
4. **Say your name like a statement, not a question.** "It's Jordan," with your voice going down, not "It's Jordan?"
5. **Pause after your question.** The pause is the skill.
6. **Practice by saying each beat 5 different ways out loud.** Then you're never stuck on exact words.
7. **Aim for dials, not yeses.** Today's goal is 21 conversations started. Any "yes" is a bonus.
